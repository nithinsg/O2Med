Six names built to last twenty years

Short, coined and easy to say, in the spirit of Inogen and ResMed. Each one below has its own logo idea, colours and type, and has been screened for domains and existing companies. The device names sit underneath the company name, so you can keep improving the product without touching the brand.

The catalogue

Screening on 14 Nov 2026: for all six, the .com, .in and "…health.com" domains were unregistered, and web and trademark-directory searches found no company or mark with the exact name. "Neighbours" are similar-sounding marks that a trademark examiner might cite. This is a first screen, not a legal clearance: a trademark attorney must search the Indian registry (classes 10, 44, 35, 9) before you commit, and the domains should be bought the same day you decide.

How I'd rank them

1. Thoraq

The punchiest, and the only one that grows with you: the thorax holds the lungs and the heart, so the name still fits when you add cardiac devices. The risk is a busy neighbourhood of "Thora-" marks.

2. Normoxa

The cleanest legally and the most meaningful to doctors: normoxia is the normal oxygen level, which is the outcome you sell. Slightly less punchy to a patient.

3. Pulmia

The warmest and easiest to say in any language. Best if the brand should feel consumer-friendly first. Locked to lungs, in a crowded "Pulm-" field.

Respeon is the closest in feel to ResMed, which helps recall and raises the chance of an objection. Vayeon and Alvayu carry the Indian root if you want that visible in the name.

Why "Stride"

It names the benefit, walking again, in one common word a patient's family understands. It reads well next to any of the six company names and leaves room for Stride 2 and 3.

Two Hindi alternatives

Sair (सैर, an outing or stroll) is short and warm. Kadam (क़दम, a step) is instantly understood across North India. Either works if you want the product, not just the service, to sound Indian.

What the label must still say

Indian rules require the real manufacturer's name and licensed model on the nameplate, with your company as importer. Your brand name should also be listed on the import licence. So the patient sees "Stride 1", and the small print names Jiayi.

Which Jiayi unit: their lightest, the 1.5 kg S1-A, isn't registered as a medical device in China, so it can't get an Indian import licence yet. The lightest one you can license today is the 2.1 kg JY-P25B, so Stride 1 is drawn on that. If Jiayi registers the S1-A, it becomes a natural Stride 1S or "Stride Mini".
