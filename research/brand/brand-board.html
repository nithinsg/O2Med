A brand for a doctor-led respiratory care company

Values first, then six screened names, three logo ideas and three colour systems. Use the bench below to try any combination before you choose.

Foundation

Promise Prescribed, titrated, monitored.

Purpose: help people with chronic lung disease breathe well enough to live their lives, at home and outside it.

Evidence before claims

Nothing goes on a box or a slide that we haven't measured or published.

Honest numbers

Oxygen in ml per minute, real battery hours, real prices. No "5 litre" talk.

We follow up

The sale is the start. Titration, usage reports and a 48-hour swap come after it.

Built for Indian conditions

Heat, dust, power cuts, altitude and family budgets are design inputs.

Clean hands

No payments for prescriptions. No shortcuts on licences or quality.

How the brand sounds

  • Calm, precise, warm, plain-spoken
  • To doctors: data, protocols, references
  • To families: short sentences, their language, no fear-selling

What it never does

  • Miracle or "hospital-grade at home" claims
  • Stock photos of smiling models in masks
  • Jargon a patient's daughter can't follow

The six names, screened

I tested about 230 candidates. Almost every obvious word (Prana, Vayu, Aeris, Respira, Breva) is taken. These six had an unregistered .com and .in today and no company using the name in web searches.

"Clear so far" means the .com and .in domains were unregistered on 13 Nov 2026 and web searches found no company or product with that name. It is not a trademark clearance. Before you commit, a trademark attorney must search the Indian registry in classes 10, 44, 35 and 9, and you should register the domains the same day you decide.

My recommendation

Name: Alvayu, with Arohiq as the alternative

Alvayu if you want the company to mean respiratory care for its first decade. Arohiq if you want a house name that stretches to cardiology or diabetes later without a rename.

Either way, name products plainly under it: Go (portable), Home (5 L unit), Care (service plan), Sense (monitoring).

Colour: Teal and Marigold

Philips and most hospital brands sit in blue. Deep teal still reads clinical, and marigold is unmistakably Indian and warm. It also prints well on a white device.

Mark: Two breaths

One shape for breathing in, one for out. It works at 16 pixels, in one colour, and moulded into plastic. The alveoli mark suits Alvayu best; the ascent mark suits Arohiq.

Type

Albert Sans for the wordmark and headings, Inter for text, Mukta for Hindi. All free, all with the weights you need for packaging and apps.

Decide in this order

1. Name

Say each one aloud to five doctors and five patients' families. Pick two. Send both to a trademark attorney.

2. Colour and mark direction

Choose on the bench above. I then build the full identity: logo files, spacing rules, device label, packaging, app icon.

3. Lock it

File the trademark, buy the domains and social handles, then brief packaging and the website.
